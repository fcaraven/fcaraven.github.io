The critical 2D Stochastic Heat Flow and KPZ process

Numerical simulations of critical 2D Stochastic Heat Flow (SHF, left) and KPZ process (right) at a fixed time. See our paper The Critical 2D KPZ Process for more details.

Spatial zoom

The SHF simulation below shows the point-to-plane partition function of a critical 2D directed polymer as a function of its starting point (locally smoothed in space, see the caption). After time reversal, the partition function solves a discretized stochastic heat equation with constant initial data, providing an approximation of the SHF at macroscopic time t = 1 with disorder parameter θ = 0. At the initial zoom, the visible region is a tilted square of macroscopic size ≈ 10×10 (the microscopic parameters are described below).

To model the SHF on the whole plane, we actually run the full simulation on a larger 12×12 domain with periodic boundary conditions. A comparison with Dirichlet boundary conditions for the same disorder shows that the plots are robust to the choice of boundary conditions.

The KPZ simulation on the right shows the (locally smoothed) logarithm of the same point-to-plane partition function. After time reversal, the log partition function gives a discrete approximation to the solution of the KPZ equation with zero initial data via the Cole–Hopf transform. To center the KPZ field, we subtract the spatial average of the log partition function at the initial zoom, averaged across all four samples. This centering acts as a renormalization, and the resulting field approximates the critical 2D KPZ process.

Spatial smoothing

The SHF and the KPZ process are random distributions (generalized functions) on the plane. We can visualize these fields as functions after local spatial smoothing, as in the simulations above. A smaller smoothing radius reveals finer local detail, while a larger radius makes large-scale spatial patterns easier to see. The animation below illustrates this tradeoff by varying the smoothing radius while keeping the zoom fixed. Seeing how the shape of the smoothed field changes with the radius conveys a fuller picture of the underlying distribution.

Parameters and scaling

We use the following parameters for the directed polymer simulation.

  • Polymer length: N = 105
  • Disorder strength: critical window with parameter θ = 0
  • Starting points: even-parity sites inside the diamond with vertices (±R√N, 0) and (0, ±R√N) with R = 5 for the initial view (the full simulation uses R = 6 to reduce boundary effects).

Dividing the microscopic domain by the diffusive scale √(N/2) (the factor 1/2 comes from the simple random walk variance in each coordinate) yields the macroscopic domain: a diamond with vertices (±√2 R, 0) and (0, ±√2 R), i.e. a tilted square of side length 2 R = 10.
